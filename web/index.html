Running it

In VICE, drag the PRG onto the window. On a C64, copy it to a disk or an SD card device and type LOAD"NAME",8 then RUN. The program picks the PAL or NTSC version for the machine. Press space to go back to BASIC.

Compare the dithers

Every dither below was tried on your picture and scored on how close the C64's version comes to the original, and the page shows its pick. Use this converts another one for PAL and NTSC, to show above and download instead.

How the scores work

SSIM compares structure (higher is closer); colour is the a*b* colour error and RMS the blurred error (lower is closer); nearby is the share of pixels the C64 cannot show exactly and draws in a nearby colour. The score weighs them against each other, 1 being the best of these dithers. In hires a quick estimate (each cell's two commonest colours, 8 sprite spans a line, no timing) scores all of them, and the best three by it are converted in full and scored again, below; the page shows the best of those. Changing the weights scores them again, and Convert again picks with them.

How it works

The picture is a hires bitmap, which allows two colours in each 8×8 cell. The pixels that do not fit are drawn with sprites that are moved and recoloured line by line as the screen is drawn: sprite rasterisation, worked out by the demo scene long ago. A raster routine made for each picture does the moving, timed to the cycle for PAL or NTSC, and the PRG holds both versions.

vic2orious is an AI-assisted project, written with Claude; I would not have got all the fiddly timing right without it. The ideas stand on the shoulders of the demo scene. Source and command-line tools: github.com/wesbiggs/vic2orious.
